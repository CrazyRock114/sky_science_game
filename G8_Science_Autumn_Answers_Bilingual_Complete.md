#### ▶ Task 1.7 — Historical Inquiry – Scurvy in Sailors
**【中文任务标题】**: 历史科学探究 — 水手长期航海患坏血病原因  
**【互动题型 / 游戏化组件】**: `open_ended` (科学论述问答题)  
**【标准依据出处】**: 《G8 Science.pdf》Chapter 1: B5.01 Diet (pp. 4-11, Table B5.01, Table B5.02, Table B5.03) | 《5008_IG Science G8_Science_Autumn (教师版PPT）.pptx》Slides 14-34  

```text
Question / Task Prompt: Explain why a sailor eating only bread and salted meat for 6 months might get scurvy.
Standard Reference Answer & Mark Scheme:
Bread and preserved salted meat contain carbohydrates, fats, and proteins, but are completely lacking in fresh fruits and
vegetables, which are the primary dietary source of Vitamin C. Vitamin C is a water-soluble micronutrient that cannot be
manufactured or stored in large amounts by the human body. Over 6 months of severe deficiency, the body loses its ability to
synthesize collagen fibres (the structural connective protein), causing blood capillaries to rupture (leading to bleeding gums and
bruising), teeth to loosen, and existing tissues to break down into clinical scurvy.
```

#### ▶ Task 1.8 — Stretch Question – Essentiality of Micronutrients
**【中文任务标题】**: 拔高拓展题 — 微量营养素需求微少却不可或缺的机制  
**【互动题型 / 游戏化组件】**: `open_ended` (深度机制推导题)  
**【标准依据出处】**: 《G8 Science.pdf》Chapter 1: B5.01 Diet (pp. 4-11, Table B5.01, Table B5.02, Table B5.03) | 《5008_IG Science G8_Science_Autumn (教师版PPT）.pptx》Slides 14-34  

```text
Question / Task Prompt: Why does the body need only small amounts of micronutrients, yet they are still essential?
Standard Reference Answer & Mark Scheme:
Micronutrients (vitamins and minerals) are not oxidized as fuel for energy, nor do they form the primary bulk structural mass of
tissues (unlike macronutrients). Instead, they act as cofactors and coenzymes that regulate biochemical reactions, facilitate
metabolic pathways, and maintain vital physiological processes (e.g., iron binding oxygen in haemoglobin, calcium enabling
muscle contraction). Because they participate in catalysed cyclical pathways and are reused, only tiny milligram or microgram
amounts are needed daily; however, in their absence, critical biochemical functions fail completely.
```

#### ▶ Task 2.3 — Core Biological Definitions (G8 Science p. 12)

```text
1. Ingestion [摄食]
Taking substances (food and drink) into the body through the mouth.
2. Digestion [消化]
The breakdown of large, insoluble food molecules into small, water-soluble molecules so that they can be absorbed into the
bloodstream.
3. Absorption [吸收]
The movement of small, digested food molecules and ions through the wall of the intestine into the blood or lymph.
4. Assimilation [同化]
The movement of digested food molecules into the cells of the body where they are used, becoming part of the cells (e.g.,
for energy, growth, and tissue repair).
5. Egestion [排遗]
The passing out of food that has not been digested or absorbed, as faeces, through the anus.
6. Mechanical Digestion [机械消化]
The physical breakdown of food into smaller pieces without chemical change to the food molecules. Examples: Chewing
(mastication) by teeth; churning of food in the stomach; emulsification of fats into small droplets by bile.
7. Chemical Digestion [化学消化]
The breakdown of large, insoluble molecules into small, soluble molecules by the catalytic action of digestive enzymes.
Examples: Salivary amylase breaking starch into maltose; pepsin in the stomach breaking proteins into peptides; lipase
breaking fats into fatty acids and glycerol.
```

```text
Chyme leaving the stomach through the pyloric sphincter is intensely acidic (pH 2). It must be promptly neutralized by alkaline
```

```text
Why is it called a 'double circulatory system'?
Because blood passes through the heart twice for each complete circuit around the body.
1. Pulmonary circuit: Deoxygenated blood is pumped from the right ventricle through the pulmonary artery to the lungs,
absorbs oxygen, and returns to the left atrium via the pulmonary veins.


2. Systemic circuit: Oxygenated blood is pumped from the left ventricle through the aorta under high pressure to all organ
tissues of the body, and returns deoxygenated blood to the right atrium via the vena cava.
Doctor Blood Pressure Challenge
Blood pressure is significantly higher in an artery than in a vein. The artery receives blood directly from the forceful contraction
of the ventricles, where pressure surges up to 120 mmHg. By the time blood navigates the dense, high-resistance networks of
millions of microscopic capillaries, friction dissipates almost all hydrostatic pressure, dropping pressure to below 10 mmHg by
the time it reaches the veins.
```

```text
mechanically through a stethoscope, and deduce its systemic effects on an individual's overall pulse pressure.
Standard Reference Answer & Mark Scheme:
Hypothesis & Acoustic Findings:
A healthy heart produces crisp 'lub-dub' sounds as valves snap shut against retrograde pressure. If an atrioventricular valve
(bicuspid/mitral or tricuspid) is incompetent and fails to close completely, high pressure generated during ventricular systole
forces blood to leak backwards into the atrium. Through a stethoscope, the crisp first sound ('lub') is replaced or followed by a

low-pressure atrium, the volume ejected into the aorta during systole is markedly decreased.
2. Drop in Systolic Arterial Pressure: Less stroke volume entering the arterial tree lowers the systolic blood pressure peak.
3. Narrowing of Pulse Pressure: Pulse pressure is the numerical difference between systolic and diastolic pressure (Pulse Pressure =
Systolic − Diastolic). As systolic pressure drops while peripheral sympathetic vascular resistance maintains or elevates diastolic
pressure, the individual experiences a significantly narrowed pulse pressure.
4. Clinical Manifestation: Poor systemic perfusion leads to chronic fatigue, low exercise tolerance, pulmonary congestion (in
mitral failure), and peripheral edema (in tricuspid failure).
```

```text
Question / Task Prompt: Compare how blood pressure is different in arteries versus veins. Explain how the built-in design
features protect arteries from bursting under high pressure, and how veins successfully push blood back up to the heart
against gravity under low pressure.
Standard Reference Answer & Mark Scheme:
Blood Pressure Comparison:
Blood in arteries is under high, pulsatile pressure (typically 80–120 mmHg in humans) generated directly by the forceful systolic
contractions of the left ventricle. In stark contrast, blood in veins is under very low, steady, non-pulsatile pressure (typically 5–10
mmHg). By the time blood passes through the high-resistance capillary beds, viscous friction dissipates almost all hydrostatic
pressure.
Arterial Protective Adaptations Against High Pressure:
1. Thick Muscular and Elastic Wall: Arteries possess a heavily developed tunica media composed of circular smooth muscle and
extensive concentric sheets of elastin protein. When the ventricle ejects blood, the elastic fibres stretch radially, damping the
destructive peak pressure spike and preventing the wall from rupturing.
2. Elastic Recoil (Windkessel Effect): During ventricular diastole, the stretched elastic wall recoils inwards against the blood,
smoothing pulsatile surges into continuous forward laminar flow and maintaining adequate diastolic perfusion pressure.
3. Tough Collagen Tunica Externa: The outer layer consists of dense collagen fibres that provide structural strength, anchoring the
vessel and limiting excessive expansion.
Venous Adaptations for Low-Pressure Upward Return Against Gravity:
1. One-Way Pocket (Semilunar) Valves: Folds of endothelium create one-way flap valves spaced along the length of veins
(particularly in the limbs). When blood flows towards the heart, the valves open freely; if blood attempts to flow backwards due
to gravity, the pocket flaps fill with blood and snap shut, preventing retrograde pooling.
2. Skeletal Muscle Pump: Deep veins run between major skeletal muscles. When muscles contract during physical movement
(such as walking), they bulge and physically compress the thin-walled veins, forcing blood upwards towards the heart.
3. Wide Lumen Design: Veins have a large internal lumen, which drastically reduces resistance to flow under minimal driving
pressure.
4. Respiratory Pump: Inhalation expands the thoracic cavity, decreasing intrathoracic pressure and pulling venous blood into the
right atrium.
```

**【中文任务标题】**: 第3步：综合归纳 / 临床重症对比与进化假说  
**【互动题型 / 游戏化组件】**: `open_ended` (探究步骤三)  
**【标准依据出处】**: 《G8 Science.pdf》Chapter 2: B7.01-B7.03 (pp. 21-27) | 《5008_IG Science G8_Science_Autumn (教师版PPT）.pptx》Cross-lesson integration  

```text
Incompetence
Question / Task Prompt: Review Figure 11.3 showing how the septum separates oxygen-rich and oxygen-poor blood. Evaluate
the clinical severity of a hole in the septum wall, judging why the resulting drop in cell oxygen delivery causes more
immediate bodily failure than a faulty valve in a leg vein:
Standard Reference Answer & Scientific Analysis:
Evaluation: A septal defect (hole in the interventricular septum) is far more clinically severe and acutely life-threatening than a
faulty valve in a peripheral vein.
Comparative Pathophysiology:
• Ventricular Septal Defect (VSD): The septum provides complete separation between oxygenated and deoxygenated blood in
a double circulation. Because left ventricular systolic pressure (~120 mmHg) is far greater than right ventricular pressure (~25
mmHg), a septal hole causes a massive left-to-right shunt. This produces severe systemic consequences:
1. Deoxygenated blood enters the systemic arterial supply, dramatically decreasing systemic arterial oxygen saturation.
2. High-pressure blood floods the pulmonary vasculature, causing severe pulmonary arterial hypertension and heart failure.
3. Vital organs with zero anaerobic tolerance (the brain and heart myocardium) are rapidly deprived of oxygen, leading to
immediate cellular hypoxia, acidosis, and circulatory arrest.
• Faulty Leg Vein Valve: In contrast, valvular incompetence in a lower limb vein causes only localized venous hypertension. While
it causes blood pooling, localized edema, and painful varicose veins, it has zero immediate effect on the chemical oxygen
saturation of arterial blood. Systemic oxygen delivery to vital organs remains completely uncompromised, making it a chronic
localized condition rather than an acute fatal systemic failure.
```

```text
Question / Task Prompt: Look at the stretching investigation in Task 15.5 (Fig 15.3) where weights were added to a segment of
a blood vessel. Evaluate the structural properties of blood vessel X based on the cross-section image in Figure 15.2. Explain
how the thick wall design links to withstanding the massive systolic pressure spikes created when the heart contracts:
Standard Reference Answer & Scientific Analysis:
Evaluation & Structural Link:
Vessel X is an artery. Its cross-section reveals an exceptionally thick wall consisting of a prominent tunica media rich in concentric
layers of elastin protein fibres and circular smooth muscle, encased by a tough outer collagenous tunica externa.
Mechanics of Withstanding Systolic Spikes:
When the left ventricle contracts forcefully during systole, it ejects roughly 70 mL of blood into the aorta in less than 0.1 seconds,
creating a violent hydraulic shock wave and a massive pressure spike (~120 mmHg).
1. Radial Elastic Stretching: Instead of acting like a rigid pipe that would fracture under sudden shock, the abundant elastic fibres
allow the arterial wall to stretch radially outward (as demonstrated in the weight-stretching investigation). This stretching expands
the vessel lumen, dampening the peak systolic pressure and preventing traumatic wall rupture.
2. Elastic Recoil during Diastole: When ventricular pressure plummets to zero during diastole, the stretched elastic fibres store
mechanical potential energy and recoil inward against the blood column. This recoil maintains a minimum driving pressure
(diastolic pressure ~80 mmHg), smoothing pulsatile bursts into a continuous forward stream of blood to downstream tissues.
```

#### ▶ Step 2: Evaluate the Capillary Exchange Link — Microvascular Slow-Motion Velocity & Single-Cell
**【中文任务标题】**: 第2步：生物力学 / 病理生理机制深度推导  
**【互动题型 / 游戏化组件】**: `open_ended` (探究步骤二)  
**【标准依据出处】**: 《G8 Science.pdf》Chapter 2: B7.03 Blood vessels (pp. 27-30) | 《5008_IG Science G8_Science_Autumn (教师版PPT）.pptx》Cross-lesson integration  

```text
Diffusion Kinetics
Question / Task Prompt: Capillaries are microscopic vessels where blood slows to a crawl and tissue walls are only one cell
thick. Evaluate why this slow-motion environment is vital for survival. Analyze how the reduction in speed and wall thickness
links to the rapid exchange of oxygen, glucose, and waste products between the blood loop and your living cells:
Standard Reference Answer & Scientific Analysis:
Evaluation & Biophysical Analysis:
The microvascular exchange environment governed by capillary architecture is governed by Fick's First Law of Diffusion: Rate of
Diffusion ∝ (Surface Area × Concentration Difference) / Diffusion Distance.
Critical Links to Survival:
1. Massive Total Cross-Sectional Area and Velocity Reduction: While an individual capillary has an ultra-narrow lumen (~7–8 µm),
the human body contains billions of branching capillaries. Their combined cross-sectional area is hundreds of times greater than
the aorta. By the continuity equation (Flow = Area × Velocity), blood velocity slows from ~400 mm/s in the aorta to less than 0.5
mm/s ('slow-motion crawl') in capillaries. This dramatic deceleration provides essential residence time (contact time) for
chemical molecules to diffuse across the barrier.
2. Single-Cell Endothelial Wall: Capillary walls consist solely of a single layer of flattened endothelial cells (~1 µm thick) supported
by a delicate basement membrane. This minimizes the diffusion distance between plasma and tissue fluid.
3. Exchange Equilibrium: Slow transit combined with a minimal diffusion barrier allows oxygen to dissociate rapidly from
haemoglobin and diffuse down its steep concentration gradient into mitochondria, while glucose diffuses into cells.
Concurrently, metabolic waste products (CO2, urea, lactic acid) diffuse rapidly into the blood for elimination.
```

#### ▶ Step 3: Evaluate the Valve and Gravity Link — Venous Valvular Failure vs. Arterial High-Pressure Physics
**【中文任务标题】**: 第3步：综合归纳 / 临床重症对比与进化假说  
**【互动题型 / 游戏化组件】**: `open_ended` (探究步骤三)  
**【标准依据出处】**: 《G8 Science.pdf》Chapter 2: B7.03 Blood vessels (pp. 27-30) | 《5008_IG Science G8_Science_Autumn (教师版PPT）.pptx》Cross-lesson integration  

```text
Question / Task Prompt: Compare the engineering of a vein to that of an artery. Evaluate the severity of a medical condition
that destroys the one-way security gates inside human veins. Judge how a failure in these valves links to blood pooling in the
lower legs due to gravity, and explain why arteries do not require these same structures to keep blood moving forward:


Standard Reference Answer & Scientific Analysis:
Evaluation & Comparative Biomechanics:
• Valvular Destruction & Chronic Venous Insufficiency: One-way semilunar valves in veins act as mechanical check-valves that
divide the vertical column of blood into short, manageable hydrostatic segments. If a disease or chronic hypertension destroys or
stretches these valves (valvular incompetence):
1. When standing, gravity exerts an uninterrupted hydrostatic downward pressure on the continuous fluid column.
2. Instead of moving upward, blood flows backwards (venous reflux) and pools under high hydrostatic pressure in the dependent
veins of the calves and ankles.
3. The resulting chronic venous hypertension distends the thin-walled superficial veins, causing them to become tortuous and
swollen (varicose veins).
4. High hydrostatic pressure forces fluid out of venules into interstitial tissue spaces, causing chronic edema, skin discoloration,
tissue necrosis, and venous stasis ulcers.
• Why Arteries Do NOT Require Valves: Arteries do not require internal valves because they are maintained under continuous
high hydrostatic pressure (mean arterial pressure ~95 mmHg) generated by the left ventricle. This powerful forward driving
pressure vastly exceeds the gravitational pull experienced anywhere in the human body, ensuring strictly unidirectional forward
flow regardless of body posture.
```
